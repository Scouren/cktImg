# Human layout choices in mirrors, bias wiring, filters and bridges

Several recognizable circuits admit more than one good drawing of the same connections. AG215 and AG1034 illustrate the consequence: their differential loads have the same six-device core, but one author crosses the gates and the other crosses the drains. Preserving the device rows alone would accept a drawing that connected the wrong output. The useful pattern includes the terminal relationships and the alternative routes a reader can follow. [AG215 original][ag215] · [AG1034 original][ag1034]

This study extends the [first pattern comparison](human-layout-patterns.md). The [coverage ledger](rule-coverage.md) reviews every identifier tagged `unmeasured` or `book` in the frozen rule inventory. The bounded searches below establish which cases match their predicates in the cache. Visual judgments are scoped to the figures actually inspected.

## Current mirrors and differential stages

| Decision | Proposed drawing convention | Evidence and limit |
|---|---|---|
| How to draw a two-tier cascode mirror | Keep the two devices in each branch in one column. Align corresponding tiers so their two separate control nets can be traced. Choose gate facing and the side used for the diode straps separately. | All 15 selected occurrences in 13 distinct upstream files have paired columns, matching tiers and the reference branch on the left. In AG287 the gates face each other. In AG390 both gates face left on each tier, while its redraw faces them inward. AG612 looks like the same two stacks but has all four gates on **one** net. [AG287][ag287] · [AG390 original][ag390] · [AG390 redraw](../../external/examples/analoggenie/ag-390.svg) · [AG612][ag612] |
| Which side takes a single mirror reference | Identify the reference from its drain-to-gate tie. Leave both left-reference and right-reference arrangements available. Preserve the branch that drives the next stage. | AG157 puts the diode-connected M3 on the left. AG242 puts M3 on the right. In AG346 the reference is on the right even though the **left** drain drives the stage farther right. In the screened redraws the split is 98 left and 32 right across 130 load relations. Those 125 decks reference 51 distinct upstream files. [AG157][ag157] · [AG242][ag242] · [AG346][ag346] |
| Where to put the crossing in a differential load | Preserve which inner PMOS gate and drain goes to each output branch. Both arrangements keep the diode-connected loads on the outside. The two inner devices can trade places. | In AG215, M3/M4 drains go to the near branches and their **gate** wires cross. In AG1034, M6/M7 gates stay with the near diode straps and their **drains** cross. A requirement to keep each mirror's gates adjacent would reject AG215. The two tail-bias circuits differ. [AG215 original][ag215] · [AG215 deck](../../external/examples/analoggenie/ag-215.cir) · [AG1034 original][ag1034] · [AG1034 deck](../../external/examples/analoggenie/ag-1034.cir) |
| How much space to give two pairs sharing drains | Preserve each private source junction, tail and input pair. The pairs may sit beside each other or one may span the other. | In AG1073, the main and auxiliary pairs sit side by side. Dashed outlines enclose the input transistors and leave the clocked tails outside. AG1078 places the main pair above and inside the width of the calibration pair. Both circuits have two distinct tails driven by the same clock and the same two drain nets shared between pairs. The netlist alone supplies neither a gap nor an enclosure. [AG1073][ag1073] · [AG1078][ag1078] · [prior membership study](image-rules/round-2/ninth-local-groups.md) |

**Selection.** The mirror search used the 2,202 AnalogGenie DUTs. For the two-tier pattern require four same-model MOS devices in two series branches, bodies tied to the common source rail of the rail-end devices, two distinct G=D reference nets, exactly the corresponding two MOS gates on each reference net, and separate nonrail copy-branch intermediate and output nets. Inspect every selected original: AG287/296/390/672/683/684/836/887/912/963/968/976/992. AG912 and AG963 contribute two occurrences each. For the single-tier search, start with a two-gate, same-rail mirror with one G=D device. Each drain must meet exactly one opposite-type MOS drain. The two input devices must have identical model/parameter tokens, common nonrail source, distinct gates and common rail body. Each input gate must differ from its own source and drain. Only AG215 and AG1034 return two such mirror loads on the *same two input devices*. AG965 shares a source among four input devices, which is insufficient. The two-pair comparison uses the earlier 24-pair cohort. [AG965][ag965] · [input-pair selection](human-layout-patterns.md#reproducing-the-comparisons)

## Bias ports and supply bars

**Distinguish the wire between gates, its branch point and the external bias endpoint.** Facing gates on a clear row often have a straight connection. In AR6241 the Q9/Q10 wire branches at the midpoint, yet the bias endpoint lies to the left of both devices. In the same original Q3/Q4 have a short central feed. In AR567 a third device occupies the interval: the VB2 gate wire goes above it and returns near M7. In AG927 the outer pair uses separate VB1 ends while the inner pair shares a central VB2 feed. AR533 is a second copy of that figure. Where separate ends are used, explicit bias text is also possible: the AG2210 redraw shows VB3 as a port at M6 and as text at the end of the M5 stub. Its upstream panel hides those devices inside an amplifier block. This qualifies the midpoint and port-symbol claims in `wires.11–13`. [AR6241][ar6241] · [AR567][ar567] · [AG927][ag927] · [AR533][ar533] · [AG2210 redraw](../../external/examples/analoggenie/ag-2210.svg)

**Choose which rail pins share a piece of wire before choosing where its name goes.** AG391 joins two sources to a VDD bar; AR6241 puts separate upward arrows on Q9 and Q10. AG295 puts individual grounds under M7/M8; AG2323(A) takes M5/M6 to the common return of the divider and load. For lettering, AG391 puts VDD on the right, AG928 above the middle and AG927 above the left portion. All three cached redraws put the name on the right. AG927 also takes a signal across the VDD bar twice with visible wire jumps. Keep clear crossing semantics and allow these text positions. The physical rail connections and the lettering need separate checks. [AG391][ag391] · [AR6241][ar6241] · [AG295][ag295] · [AG2323][ag2323] · [AG928][ag928] · [AG927][ag927]

**Selection.** Across 5,274 cached drawings, select a DUT formal beginning VB or BIAS, with exactly two device incidences, both MOS gates on the same row facing each other. The 194 nets in 120 redraws divide into 189 straight spans, one continuous detour (AR567:VB2), and four separated pairs (AG716:VB1, AG927:VB1, AR533:VB1, AG2210:VB3). The gate pin bindings were checked against the decks. For the subset with same-type devices, level source pins and both S and B on the same named rail, trace physical conductor connectivity between source tips. Of 51 VDD pairs, 47 are joined and four separate. Of 36 VSS pairs, seven are joined and 29 separate. Selection on the existing gate pose limits both counts to that pose. They provide no general distance threshold for splitting a rail. [Source cache and terminal-table provenance](../../external/examples/README.md)

## Passive two-ports

| Circuit | What to preserve | Where a simpler rule fails |
|---|---|---|
| Three-section ladder | Keep the three through elements on a common row in tap order. Draw the return branches perpendicular to that row. | In AR5432 inductors L2/L4 lie along the signal row, while L1/L3/L5 stand on its branches. The four exact matches agree on the axes. AG458, outside this set, has two output paths from one input and shows return branches above and below. [AR5432][ar5432] · [AR1910][ar1910] · [AG458][ag458] |
| Bridged T | Keep the two-element path with the shunt tap on the port row. Put the direct element between the *outer* ports on a separate run. | In AR6363 the overhead element is R3; in AR6364 it is C3. Selecting the shortest component path puts that one element on the main row in both drawings and relegates the junction that explains the T to a detour. The cases support the relation; they leave its vertical reflection open. [AR6363][ar6363] · [AR6364][ar6364] |
| Parallel LC | Give the inductor and capacitor separate paths joining the same two endpoints. Choose their common direction with the branch they occupy. | AR6358 and AR6360 put both elements horizontally between the driven and output-side nodes. AR6361 stands them upright from a differential readout node to the common return. A two-terminal parallel tank alone settles neither page direction nor which part comes first. [AR6358][ar6358] · [AR6360][ar6360] · [AR6361][ar6361] |

**Selection.** Screen all 5,274 converted DUT decks for exactly three distinct formals, including VSS, and only two-terminal R/L/C cards. With `a,b` the other formals in either order and `x,y` distinct internal nodes, require the entire edge multiset. For the ladder it is `a–x, x–y, y–b, x–VSS, y–VSS, b–VSS`, which returns AR1910/5431/5432/6308. For the bridged T it is `a–x, x–b, x–VSS, a–b`, which returns AR6363/6364. All six originals and redraws were inspected. The LC and shared-input comparisons are named controls. AR6375 gives a further limit on comparing wire partitions: the original two-port has a drawn lower return conductor joining its reference contacts, while the redraw uses a ground symbol. ## Four-diode bridges, differential voltages and annotations

**A four-diode connection can suggest a bridge arrangement. Its use and level of detail decide how to draw it.** Look for two anode-to-cathode paths of two ordinary diodes each, sharing start and finish but having distinct middle nodes. Keep all four nets distinct. This directed pattern occurs once in each of eleven cached DUTs. The originals give four different ways of presenting those four positions.

| Upstream presentation | Cases | What changes in the gallery |
|---|---|---|
| Four oblique diode bodies around a diamond | AR645/719/723/3661/5661/5671 | AR645 keeps the diamond. The other five redraws use upright columns. AR645 puts its R and C load outside; AR719 shows the load between the opposed corners. AR723 uses the middle vertices for signal in/out and feeds the other two vertices from bias resistors. [AR645][ar645] · [AR719][ar719] · [AR723][ar723] · [AR5671][ar5671] |
| Four contacts on a diamond with a single diode icon inside | AR649/1485 | Both redraws expand it to four diagonal diodes. In AR649 the three additional series diodes beside the load stay explicit even in the original. The compact drawing communicates a function; it supplies no evidence for changing device identity, model, or packaging. [AR649 original][ar649] · [redraw](../../external/examples/analogretriever/ar-649.svg) · [AR1485][ar1485] |
| Two columns of upright diodes within a sampling stage | AG763/1042 | The redraws keep the columns. The hold capacitor and buffer attach to a middle vertex. A rule that turns every matching subgraph into a rectifier symbol would conceal that signal path. [AG763][ag763] · [AG1042][ag1042] |
| Two horizontal tuning branches | AG1142 | The upstream symbols at Vtune+ and Vtune− are varactors. The converted deck calls all four ordinary DIODE. The positions correspond, but the converted cards do not establish the specialized glyph or a rectifying function. [AG1142][ag1142] · [deck](../../external/examples/analoggenie/ag-1142.cir) |

**Use a diamond as an arrangement option for explicit members.** Preserve the cathode end of each diode and every external attachment. Count AR719 as shape evidence only: the original prints D3's cathode toward the upper AC vertex, while its deck puts the cathode at the right-hand load vertex. A geometry-only acceptance check would miss that disagreement. AR646 supplies the converse boundary: a voltage source replaces one diode in its diagram and deck while the drawing retains the diamond. A similar outline can be useful across device types without authorizing a four-diode substitution. [AR719 original][ar719] · [AR719 deck](../../external/examples/analogretriever/ar-719.cir) · [AR646 original][ar646] · [AR646 deck](../../external/examples/analogretriever/ar-646.cir)

**Leave room for an explicitly specified differential measurement between its two nodes.** In AR4183 the two open sense terminals face inward, with positive on the left. In AR719 the signs sit above the horizontal load, with positive on the right. In AR5341 the interior is occupied by an actual resistor between A and B. The cards distinguish an open readout from a connected load. They may omit the measurement annotation: AR719 exports only Vin+ and Vin−, while AR4183 exports both sense nets but supplies four nominal 1k resistors where the original labels opposite arms `R + ΔR` and `R − ΔR`. Preserve supplied signs, values and the space needed for them. Recovering the author's measurement or varying resistance requires evidence beyond those card values. [AR4183 original][ar4183] · [deck](../../external/examples/analogretriever/ar-4183.cir) · [AR719][ar719] · [AR5341][ar5341]

**Selection.** All 5,274 terminal tables were searched for the directed pattern before looking at pose, names or surrounding circuitry. A second search over `.subckt dut` cards with the ordinary DIODE model returned the same eleven IDs. All eleven originals and redraws were compared visually. In the redraws the four glyph axes are diagonal in three, vertical in seven and horizontal in one. The six upstream diamonds with individual diodes are a geometry count, subject to the AR719 polarity disagreement. AR646, AR4183 and AR5341 are outside this denominator. Figures come from the pinned [AnalogRetriever release][ardata] and [AnalogGenie collection][agdata]. IDs count examples, not independent authors.

## What remains open

The evidence now supports alternatives for group orientation, branch placement, bias and rail wiring, and reading a differential output. It still leaves consequential page decisions open: how much extra space to give a dense group for names, when to continue a wire onto another shelf, and which of two equally legible arrangements to choose when the surrounding stage or the author's purpose is unknown. A useful comparison for those questions holds the terminal relationships fixed and records *where the reader enters the group, what they need to trace, and what physically occupies the proposed space*. A single global score for wire length or crossings cannot answer all three. [Unmeasured and book-based claims, with outstanding evidence](rule-coverage.md)
