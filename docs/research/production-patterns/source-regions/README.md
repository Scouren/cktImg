## Scope and verification

The diagnostic checks one original subcircuit with up to 128 devices in the supported ordered M/R/L/C/I/V/D grammar. The source’s device names, pin order and parameter tokens must all agree with the actual parsed graph. Each bridge needs a distinct, positive, same-valued DC current branch from the appropriate rail with the correct polarity and ordered ends. Two arms require equal values and the drain of a same-model MOS whose source and body reach the appropriate rail. Every original occurrence on the private nets must be accounted for. Candidate output carries no coordinate request.

Across 2,202 named AnalogGenie decks, 1,146 cells passed this source boundary, 1,030 were outside the grammar and 26 failed source-to-graph binding. The 1,498 retained regions match the frozen study on exact source members and every ordered terminal incidence. Of those rows, 1,123 were direct-source pairs, 357 had a source count other than two, 11 resistor-bearing rows remained unresolved and seven had certified attachments. The study’s broader 1,983-region inventory includes unsupported production grammars. The reference is the original planning workspace’s `docs/research/image-rules/round-2/groups_census.json`. [Independent exact ownership and mutation tests](../../../../tests/source_regions.py)

Run `nix develop -c zig build parent-census -- external/examples/analoggenie/ag-459.cir` to inspect the two ordered attachment records. In the regression, a source-body intrusion, a capacitor on the private node, a current source spanning the two regions, unequal resistor arms and mismatched current magnitudes or directions withdraw the affected claim. A four-terminal BJT causes an explicit unsupported scope. Device renaming, internal-net renaming and reversing all device cards preserve the complete records across all six circuits.
